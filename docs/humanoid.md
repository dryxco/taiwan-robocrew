# Unitree G1 stationary food-work embodiment

`server/humanoid.py` provides `augment_scene(xml)` and `HumanoidController(model, data)` with the existing dual-arm controller API. The complete official Unitree G1 model includes its pelvis, torso, head, both arms, hands, legs and feet. A white chef toque follows the rigid head/torso frame.

Upstream MJCF and all 35 meshes are unchanged under `server/assets/g1`; `LICENSE`, original README and `UPSTREAM.txt` preserve Unitree attribution and the exact Google DeepMind Menagerie revision. Runtime changes remove the floating base and leg/waist joints, fix a 20-degree working lean, remove robot collision geoms, add position servos and gravity compensation, and attach simple sliding tool fingers. **Fixed base, no walking or balance control; no hardware control.** Food/fixture contacts remain physical; robot self-contact and robot/fixture collision avoidance are not implemented.

The pelvis is fixed at `[-.04, .25, .793]`, facing world -Y. This is a compact demonstration workcell: the torso is behind the task fixtures, but a deep solid counter extending behind y=.25 would intersect the fixed lower body. Counter geometry should end before the robot footprint or use an open employee-side work surface. Meshes and limb lengths are unscaled. The TCP sits 12cm along the wrist hand direction; simulated grasp fingers are additions, not official Unitree hand hardware.

`first_person` is attached just forward of the head and looks down the counter; `operator` is on the customer side and frames the full body, subject to natural counter occlusion. `wrist` follows the left TCP. The first food freejoint and its qpos remain first; existing equalities retain their indices and `grasp` binds the actual left TCP. `right_grasp` is appended. Explicit grasp uses the inherited proximity and single-owner checks.

The preserved weld places the food center **7cm below TCP**. Thus food at `[-.231,-.0825,.77]` needs TCP target `[-.231,-.0825,.84]`. With upright tool orientation, measured left solve residual is about 6.8mm at that source TCP; lift `[-.231,-.0825,1.1]`, seasoning `[0,.132,1.08]`, bag `[.209,.11,.84]`, pickup `[.2365,-.187,.84]`, and oven `[0,.03025,.9]` solve below 0.02mm. Direct TCP z=.77 at the source is unreachable (about 53mm residual), and is explicitly rejected. These are kinematic reach checks, not collision-free trajectory guarantees or a hardware workspace specification.

`step()` computes IK on scratch data and changes only actuator commands; physics advances with the caller's `mj_step`. `reset()` initializes robot joints only. Seven joint targets per arm are bounded and slewed at 1.5rad/s. Unreachable commands hold the previous servo target and report `unreachable_pose`. Both arms accept independent positions and optional wxyz orientations. The compatibility `twist` argument is validated but does not overwrite a wrist joint; callers should use an explicit orientation for G1 wrist rotation.

Validation: `python -m pytest server/tests/test_humanoid.py` covers model loading, mesh/body presence, original food indices, workspace limits, both-arm physical actuation, rejection behavior and actual MuJoCo camera renders. No production deployment is part of this module change.
